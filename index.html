RESEARCH PAPER · 2027AS LISTED IN CV

Post-Quantum Authentication and Adversarial Robustness Evaluation for Secure CNN-Based Image Classification Model

Ayantika Gangopadhyay, Joy Chakraborty

This paper introduces PQCML-Secure, a framework that protects AI image models from both pixel-tweaking adversarial attacks (FGSM) and file tampering during deployment. By combining robust adversarial retraining with a lightweight Dilithium-inspired post-quantum authentication system, the architecture successfully defends model integrity and maintains high classification accuracy. Tested on geometric and MNIST datasets, the system blocks hackers from fooling predictions or altering model files, offering a fast and highly secure pipeline for real-world AI deployment.

Springer Nature Cureus JournalPost-Quantum AuthenticationAdversarial Robustness
CONFERENCE PAPER · 2026AS LISTED IN CV

Optimisation for the Number Theoretic Transform in ML-KEM on Apple M2 Architecture

Ayantika Gangopadhyay

This paper optimizes the critical Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) operation in ML-KEM (FIPS 203) by fusing layers 5 and 6 into a single execution step. By keeping intermediate data entirely inside the CPU registers rather than bouncing it back and forth to main memory, this approach eliminates cache bottlenecks without altering the underlying cryptographic math. When benchmarked over 100,000 iterations on an Apple M2 processor, this register-level optimization achieves a localized performance boost of 7.27% while maintaining flawless arithmetic correctness.

National Institute of Technology Goa1st International Conference on Mathematics and AI: Modelling, Analysis and Computation (ICMAI-MAC 2026)
BOOK CHAPTER · 2026AS LISTED IN CV

Machine Learning-Based Prediction and Classification of Arsenic Contamination in Water

Ayantika Gangopadhyay, Debasree Mitra, Riya Rabidas, Ankita Das

This study develops a cost-effective machine learning framework to predict groundwater arsenic contamination in India using easily measurable water parameters like pH, turbidity, and temperature. By testing four algorithms on a realistic groundwater dataset, the research demonstrates that ensemble methods like Random Forest and Gradient Boosting deliver the highest predictive accuracy. Ultimately, this approach serves as a scalable early-warning system for rural public health monitoring and future IoT integration.
